# Waveform Design: Optimal Waveform Design for Dual-Function MIMO Radar Communication System [with MATLAB Code]

Introduction: Spectrum is one of the most valuable resources in wireless communication. Realizing the scarcity of spectrum, network providers and policy regulators are exploring the feasibility of sharing spectrum currently occupied by other applications with LTE and Wi-Fi systems in the near future [1]-[4], such as airborne radar and navigation systems near the 3.4 GHz band, and shipborne and Vessel Traffic Service (VTS) radar at 5.6 GHz. As an emerging research topic, Communication-Radar Spectrum Sharing (CRSS) not only effectively utilizes spectrum resources but also provides a new approach for the co-design of radar and communication systems.
A simple method to achieve spectrum coexistence between communication and radar systems is opportunistic spectrum sharing [7]. However, it does not allow two systems to operate simultaneously. In view of this, many works [8]- [13] consider zero spatial projection (NSP) schemes for different scenarios of coexistence between radar and communication systems. Here, the radar beamformer is designed to project signals onto the zero-spatial of interference channels between the radar and base station (BS)/user equipment (UE), making the interference from the radar to the communication link zero. However, this leads to performance loss for the radar because beamforming is no longer optimal for target detection and estimation. Recent contributions [14]-[18] have utilized techniques that optimize by relaxing precoding constraints to impose controllable levels of interference on the communication system, achieving a compromise between radar and communication performance [9], providing a more realistic coexistence.
The key shortcomings of the aforementioned coexistence method lie in the fact that radar and communication equipment often need to exchange auxiliary information for beneficial collaboration, such as channel state information, radar detection waveforms, and communication modulation formats. This leads to considerable additional complexity in the system and thus makes it difficult to implement in real-world scenarios. The new dual-function radar communication (RadCom) system is considered an advantageous CRSS approach to overcome this deficiency. As a enabling solution, dual-function waveform design can support both target detection and information transmission simultaneously. Single antenna systems [19]-[22] have explored this possibility, where several integrated waveforms have been proposed to harvest radar and communication signals jointly. Additionally, [23] and [24] consider waveform mixing or phase-shift keying (PSK) through different beamformer weighting factors on the antenna. The common feature of these methods is that a communication symbol is represented by one or more radar pulses, resulting in low data rates at the radar pulse repetition frequency (PRF) level.
This paper contributes two novel dual-function MIMO RadCom system waveform designs based on optimized optimization. First, we consider waveform design under specific radar constraints ensuring optimal radar performance by minimizing downlink MUI. Next, we consider the trade-off optimization between radar and communication performance under power budget constraints. Although non-convex, both problems are effectively solved. It is worth noting that the proposed methods generate proven global optimal waveforms for simultaneous target detection and downlink communication. Simulation results indicate that the trade-off optimization method achieves a good balance between communication and radar performance.
